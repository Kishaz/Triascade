## Research-use statement

We are developing the Immune Cascade Index (ICI), a literature-derived transcriptomic model that
decomposes the anti-tumor immune response into three biological layers — antigen sensing,
regulatory integration, and cytotoxic execution — scored from bulk tumor RNA. In randomized
immune-checkpoint-blockade trials we have found that the balance within the integration layer
(activating recruitment versus stromal/angiogenic and regulatory suppression) identifies the
patients who benefit specifically from adding checkpoint blockade, and that non-responders separate
into distinct, mechanistically interpretable classes.

A central hypothesis is that the integration-suppressed, angiogenic/CAF-high non-responder class
should benefit from adding a VEGF-pathway agent. IMmotion150 and IMmotion151 are the appropriate
test: randomized trials of atezolizumab with and without bevacizumab versus sunitinib, with
pre-treatment tumor transcriptomes and response/survival outcomes. We will:

1. Score each tumor's three ICI layers and its limiting-layer class (no patient-level
   re-identification is required or attempted).
2. Test whether the integration-suppressed / angiogenic-high class derives preferential benefit
   from the bevacizumab-containing arm (a treatment-by-biomarker interaction), and benchmark the
   ICI against established signatures (Angio, Teff) already reported for these cohorts.

No attempt will be made to identify individual participants. Results will be reported only in
aggregate. We request the processed expression matrices (TPM/counts), the randomized arm, and the
response/survival endpoints; we do not require raw sequence (FASTQ) data unless the DAC prefers to
release only raw data, in which case we can process it.
